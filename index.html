Why this lesson matters

"Is this vegetarian?" is one of the most useful questions at yum cha, because so many dishes hide meat or seafood in the filling or sauce.

🎯 Interaction purpose

You tell the waitress you're vegetarian and ask what vegetarian dim sum they have. You check whether the turnip cake is vegetarian. She isn't sure, so she checks with the kitchen. It isn't, so you choose another dish.

✅ By the end you can…

  • Say you're vegetarian: 我食素 ngo5 sik6 sou3
  • Name what you don't eat: 我唔食… ngo5 m4 sik6…
  • Ask if a dish is vegetarian: …係咪素㗎？ hai6 mai6 sou3 gaa3?
  • Respond when staff say 我唔肯定 ngo5 m4 hang2 ding6 and need to check
Completion goal: state that you are vegetarian, ask whether one dish is vegetarian, and respond when staff need to check with the kitchen.

ℹ️ This lesson supports vegetarian conversations only. It is not allergy-safety training and does not guarantee that a dish is suitable.

🍵 Celia's tip

Besides 食素 sik6 sou3, 食齋 sik6 zaai1 is another common way to say that you eat vegetarian food. 食齋 can sometimes suggest Buddhist-style vegetarian food, while some speakers use it more generally. Meanings can vary, so it is still helpful to name the ingredients you do not eat.

In Hong Kong, 食素 can be interpreted in different ways. Say clearly whether you eat eggs or dairy, and name ingredients such as 蠔油 hou4 jau4, 魚露 jyu4 lou6 or 蝦米 haa1 mai5 that you need to avoid. Asking staff to check with the kitchen is normal and helpful.

  • Recipes vary. A vegetable name does not guarantee that the filling, stock, sauce, or cooking method is vegetarian.
  • Staff may need to check the kitchen, and the answer can differ by restaurant or by day.
  • This lesson is for vegetarian language practice. It does not cover allergies, cross-contact, medical risk, or guaranteed-safe foods.

3. What does Maya order instead?
